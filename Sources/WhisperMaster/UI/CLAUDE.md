# UI (`Sources/WhisperMaster/UI/`)

Design-system and notch-surface rules for this directory. The component spec is
`docs/07-design-system.md` (§8a normative for buttons); tokens live in `Theme.swift`.

### UI

- **Quick actions on hover** (`UI/QuickActions/`) — resting the pointer on the notch opens a band holding **today** and the notes you touched last, plus the three things you'd otherwise open the window for. **The left column is the day, not a reminders list** (`NotchDayTimelineColumn` over `NowTimeline`): events and reminders interleaved by time, past dimmed rather than dropped, an honest "N more today" instead of a silent truncation. It used to be a reminders column beside a notes column, which answered two thirds of "what's on my plate" and left the calendar — which the app already reads for the spoken day summary — out of the one surface built for that question. The notes column keeps a **fixed** narrower measure (`notesColumnWidth`): splitting the band evenly gave three note titles the same room as five timeline rows. Four pieces: `NotchHoverGesture` (pure dwell/grace recogniser, unit-tested — the notch is crossed constantly on the way to the menu bar, so it opens only after the pointer *rests* for `openDwell` and closes only after `closeGrace` off it, which is what covers the gap a hand crosses on the way down into the band), `NotchQuickActionsModel` (what the columns hold + `canOpen`), `NotchQuickActionsView`, `NotchQuickActionsWindow`. Two rules make it safe to put a panel on the notch: **it is only on screen while open** — nothing of ours sits over the menu bar otherwise, so a click up there still reaches the menu bar exactly as before — and **hover is polled, not tracked** (`NSEvent.mouseLocation` on a 0.06 s tick against `NotchQuickActionsLayout.hoverZone`; with no panel on screen there is no view to receive `mouseEntered`, and a tracking area on a panel that resizes under the cursor doesn't reliably re-fire).
  - **The target is a block, not a strip, and the tick is part of the feel.** The zone is the notch body plus `hoverSideExtension` wings, running from the top of the screen down through the menu bar and `approachDepth` past it. Both halves of that were the "it only answers at the notch's bottom edge" bug: a 10pt wing meant aiming at the camera housing itself, and a zone only as deep as the notch missed a pointer that decelerated to rest a few points *under* the menu bar, which is where a hand travelling up naturally stops. It stops short of the menu bar's own items either side — reaching for the clock must not open it — and `NotchQuickActionsZoneTests` pins both edges of that trade. The poll interval is pure latency on top of the dwell (the pointer arrives *between* samples), so it's small: comparing a point against two rects costs nothing, and at 0.15 s a third of a second of slop read as the notch ignoring you. While the panel is open, `isPointerEngaged` unions the band (plus `exitMargin`) with the notch zone, so the whole travel from notch to band keeps it up and the grace is a safety net rather than the thing holding it open. It yields the notch to the dictation surface via `AppState.notchIsOccupied` (generous on purpose — when in doubt dictation wins) and to onboarding via `suspend()`/`resume()` from the AppDelegate. **It composes nothing itself**: a non-activating panel on the bezel has no key window, so "New note"/"New reminder" set `AppState.requestedNotesComposer` and the real editor opens in Settings. Depth is content-driven (`thickness(rows:)`) so an empty account gets a strip, not a slab. Its one inline mutation — the reminder checkbox — **ticks both ways**: a row ticked during a glance stays in place, struck through, until the panel closes, so a mis-click is undoable on the bezel (`NotchQuickActionsModel.ticked` holds the pre-tick snapshots; see **The notch checkboxes** in the root `CLAUDE.md` for why an id isn't enough). Hard off-switch: `AppState.quickActionsEnabled` (**on by default**). Neither this nor `nowSurfaceEnabled` has a Settings row — both are defaults-only keys, which is the pre-existing shape for this surface.
- **Onboarding happens in the notch** (`UI/Onboarding/`), not in a window — first-run setup is presented on exactly the surface dictation will live on, so the user learns where to look while they're granting. `NotchOnboardingWindow` is a borderless `.nonactivatingPanel` anchored top-center (same level / collection behavior / pinned-dark appearance as `DictationPillWindow`, but it **takes clicks** and is sized exactly to its one fixed surface via `NotchOnboardingLayout`); it hosts `NotchOnboardingView`, a molded `NotchShape` band that drops out of the notch holding one ask at a time: step dots, the orb, one headline, one sub-line, one button (plus gear → Settings and × → dismiss). Four beats (`NotchOnboardingStep`): **microphone** → **accessibility** → **open at login** → **ready** (the shortcut + the hand-off). The login-item beat is here rather than only in Settings because a shortcut that does nothing after a reboot reads as the app being broken; it treats `.requiresApproval` as its own ask (see **Open at login** in the root `CLAUDE.md`) and never counts it as done. `NotchOnboardingModel` (`@MainActor @Observable`, owned by the window) holds the flow: it polls the two grants (neither posts a change notification — 0.75 s tick + `didBecomeActive`, same as the old wizard), auto-advances a beat after each ask is satisfied, and runs the mic check.
  - **The orb is the flow's only moving part — there is no waveform.** It **thinks** (`solving`) while an ask is outstanding, **works** (`orbits`) while a system prompt is up, and **listens** (`wave`, driven by the real mic level) once it can hear. That's what makes the microphone beat proof instead of a claim: it won't auto-advance on the grant alone, it waits until it has actually heard a voice (`heardVoice`), with a "Continue" ghost for anyone who'd rather not speak. `OrbView` gained a `preset` knob for this — the notch band uses the inline-scale `small` tuning, the onboarding hero uses `large`.
  - The mic check owns **its own `MicrophoneCaptureService`** (each one owns an `AVAudioEngine`, so it must not share the view model's) and runs **only** during the microphone beat, which is the one place the copy says we're listening; `close()`/`teardown()` releases it. `AppDelegate.showOnboarding` **hides the dictation pill** while the band is up (both anchor to the notch, and the launch-time engine download would otherwise draw its progress band straight through this one) and `dismissOnboarding()` gives it back.
  - Persistence is unchanged: `OnboardingStep`/`OnboardingProgress` still record "this account has onboarded" (per-Clerk-user, with the legacy-id migration), so `NotchOnboardingStep` is presentation only. Voice engine is **not** part of the flow — `prepareDefaultEngineOnLaunch()` runs post-auth in parallel and only downloads when `TranscriberEngine.isInstalled` is false; the last beat just tells the truth about its progress instead of claiming "all set".
- **Notes & Reminders is a canvas beside a list** (`Settings/NotesSettingsView.swift`, `Settings/StickyNoteCard.swift`). Three surfaces behind one sidebar item, switched by `NotesTab` (`overview` / `notes` / `reminders`): **Overview** shows both halves side by side (notes flexible, reminders a fixed 330pt column) because "what did I write down / what's coming up" is one mental space; **Notes** is the full sticky canvas; **Reminders** is the list plus the alert defaults. The asymmetry is deliberate — a note is re-read by *scanning* (so it gets a stable tint and a card), a reminder is read by *time* (so it gets one ordered column).
  - **Completion moves the row; it doesn't just cross it out.** The Reminders tab is two lists — what's left (`store.activeReminders`) and a collapsed **Completed** archive (`store.completedReminders`, newest-finished first) — because a strikethrough left in due-date order meant done work went on competing for attention with work that wasn't. Ticking a one-off **expands the archive** as it moves, so the row is watched out of one list into another rather than appearing to be deleted, and the undo is where the user just looked. The archive header carries its count while collapsed, offers **Clear** (confirmed; soft-deletes) while open, and renders at most `archiveDisplayLimit` rows with an honest "N older not shown" line rather than silently truncating. The Overview column and the tab badge both read `activeReminders` — a preview of five slots must not spend one on something already done.
  - **The checkbox leads the row and ticks both ways**, the same affordance the notch banner and quick-actions panel use, and un-ticking goes through `restoreReminder(_ snapshot:)` with the row's own copy for the reason spelled out in the root `CLAUDE.md`. It used to be one of three identical grey glyphs in a *trailing* cluster, which on an 820pt row put "done" a window-width from the title it applied to. A **repeating** reminder never archives, so its checkbox says so up front ("Mark this one done — next <date>") and ticking it re-dates the row in place. The second line is the row's one piece of colour: `Overdue · <date>` in `Theme.danger`, `Next · <date>` for repeats, and a relative "Done 20 minutes ago" in the archive.
  - **`NotesTab` lives in `SettingsView`, not in `AppState`.** The sidebar's Notes group expands into three sub-rows that select the same value the page's own tab bar drives, so a `@State` above both is what keeps them from disagreeing; routing it through `AppState` would also mean a view writing state the view model owns. The sub-rows are deliberately *subordinate* chrome (indent rule + smaller type, not the parent's pill-and-glow) — matching the parent's treatment made the sidebar read as seven peers instead of four sections with one open.
  - **The notes page is the one section with a wider measure** (`SettingsView.contentMaxWidth` → 1180 vs the 780 reading measure). At 780 the overview split collapses to one sticky per row with the reminders squeezed beside it.
  - **`StickyNoteCard` keeps two house rules the sticky metaphor would break:** nothing is **rotated** (§6, and rotated text is measurably harder to read), and the tints are **desaturated paper** rather than highlighter yellow — ember and signal are spoken for (§1), and five loud squares would shout down the window. `Theme.Sticky` holds them; **index order is load-bearing** because `Note.colorIndex` is *stored* (a `UUID.hashValue`-derived colour would reshuffle every launch — the hash is seeded per process). Append to that array, never reorder.
  - **A spoken note carries what was said and how it sounded.** `Note.transcript` is the verbatim capture and `Note.audio` names a WAV in `NoteAudioStore`; the card offers playback plus a "What I heard" disclosure. `distinctTranscript` hides the transcript when it matches the body, because the assistant often leaves them identical and the same sentence twice under a "what I heard" label reads as a bug. Audio is **not synced** (the transcript is text the user chose to store; the voice stays on the Mac that recorded it), so a note pulled from another Mac renders the affordance but can't promise playback — `NoteAudioStore.playableURL` is what answers that honestly.
  - **Pinning is the only path from a note to the bezel.** A pinned note leads `NotesStore.visibleNotes` (pin beats recency everywhere), gets its own labelled band on the canvas, and appears in the quick-actions notes column with an ember pin, a waveform glyph if it has audio, and an unpin affordance — the surface that shows a pin has to offer the way off, or undoing it means walking to the window.
- `SettingsView` — the **main window** (`SettingsView.swift`). A floating frosted-glass panel (`.glassPanel`) over `WarmBackground`, with a slim sidebar (brand lockup, four nav pills, a push-to-talk mic card, the account row) beside a scrolling content pane. The sidebar is **four primary sections** — Today / Notes & Reminders / Connectors / Settings (`SettingsSection.primary`); the six former sections (Insights, Voice engine, Traces, Permissions, Nearby Macs, About = `SettingsSection.secondary`) are folded into a "More" list on the Settings screen and open as sub-pages with a back affordance (a secondary page highlights `settings` in the sidebar via `sidebarParent`). **A section that is not released keeps its row and reads as a roadmap entry** — dimmed, inert, and carrying a `RowTag("Soon")` — in the sidebar *and* in the More list, and its page renders `ComingSoonPanel`. `SettingsSection.isAvailable` is the one answer all three read: Connectors and Notes ride on `FeatureFlags.connectorsAndNotesAvailable`; **Nearby Macs is closed on every channel** (on hold). `RowTag` is also the "Experimental" tag on a settings row — one definition, so the two can't drift. **There is no Account page** — the sidebar's bottom account row carries a gear button to the right of the avatar/name that opens `AccountPopover` (`Settings/AccountPopover.swift`: email, account id in small mono under it, joined date, sign out with an inline confirm — a `confirmationDialog` would dismiss the popover). Clerk state is read the same way as `AuthGateView` (`@Environment(Clerk.self)` on a live-only subview, snapshot path uses a static stand-in; `AccountIdentity` derives name/email/avatar for both the row and the popup). **A waiting update is announced in the sidebar, and installed from it** — `SidebarUpdateCard` sits above the mic card and is drawn only while `AppState.availableUpdateVersion` is set, which the AppDelegate's `SPUUpdaterDelegate` writes from the **silent** `checkForUpdateInformation()` fired post-auth (held behind the gate because `allowedChannels` reads the signed-in user's beta flag). The tap runs the ordinary `checkForUpdates` action, so Sparkle still owns the release notes, the download, the signature check and the relaunch. The same value drives the popup's one non-destructive button, which reads **Check for updates** with the running version beside it and flips to a solid-ember **Install update** with the new one — solid rather than tinted because the sign-out button directly under it is danger-red on a pale wash, and an ember tint beside it read as a second warning. Renders: `panel-account-popover{,-update}`, and `seedMockData` sets a mock version so the card is in every `window-*` snapshot. `TodayView` is the greeting + **real-data-only** cards (live EventKit agenda via `CalendarConnector`/`DaySummaryService`, due reminders from `NotesStore`); no weather/mail/AI-briefing (no backend). When the model isn't installed at launch, `autoFocusSetupIfNeeded` jumps to the Engine sub-page; `SetupBanner` renders atop panels while prep is in flight.
- `DictationPillWindow` / `DictationPillContent` — floating pill showing audio level + transcription state on a pitch-black notch band. **The band follows the pointer's display** (`PointerScreen`) — dictation, onboarding, and quick actions all sit on the screen `NSEvent.mouseLocation` is in, not the first notched one. A notchless external still gets the band at top-center. Reposition is change-guarded on `NSScreenNumber` so a follow tick that finds the pointer still there is a no-op. `state.hidePillWhenIdle` controls visibility between recordings. **The band does NOT show the live transcript.** It reports the *state* of a dictation, never the words as they stream: reading your own speech back off the bezel pulls your eyes away from whatever you're dictating into, and the words are already landing there. So `DictationPillContent.transcriptModel` is empty for the whole recording → finalizing → polishing stretch — which is what keeps the surface in its slim menu-bar row (`isNotchRow`) for an entire dictation — and non-empty **only** for the finished polished beat. Don't re-wire `state.transcript` into it "so you can see what it heard"; the Traces page and the target app are where the words live. **The bar's two ends are fixed and never swap:** the state in words sits at the **leading** edge — "Dictating", or **"Dictating (hands-free)"** in toggle mode, where the band staying open isn't explained by a held key (`DictationStatusView.liveStateWord`, also the VoiceOver label) — and the `OrbView` at the **trailing** edge (40pt, `NotchTranscriptRow.orbDiameter`, which `bottomThickness` tracks so a lone orb is never clipped). The light behind the orb is `NotchGlow`'s, not the row's (below).

  **The ambient slot: the day shares the row rather than losing it.**
  `NotchNowRow` puts the one thing `NowRelevance` picked (see
  `Sources/WhisperMaster/Now/CLAUDE.md`) in the **leading** wing, and when it is
  present the app's own state word demotes to a `NotchStateChip` beside the orb.
  That is a deliberate break from "the bar's two ends are fixed and never swap",
  and it is the whole point of the change: the state word owned the leading edge,
  so every machine state — a dictation, an agent mid-turn — pushed a meeting three
  minutes away off the bezel entirely. **The swap only happens while there is
  something ambient to say**; with no item the row is byte-identical to what it
  always was, which is what keeps every existing `pill-*` snapshot valid.
  - **It is the lowest rung there is**, and it needs no rule of its own to stay
    there: it is drawn only in the *row* form, so any banner that drops the band
    takes the whole surface. It is not in `bandIsBanner`, not in `notchIsOccupied`
    (the hover panel must still open over it), and it never delays anything.
    `showAmbientOnly` is the notch at rest with a day — one line, no orb, and the
    Join pill riding the trailing wing where the orb would be, because a bar with
    everything bunched against one end reads as a slab.
  - **⚠️ It is inert apart from one control, and that is load-bearing.** The row
    can be up for the ten minutes before a meeting, so the window is interactive
    for that whole stretch — which is why `DictationPillContent` marks the black
    fill `.allowsHitTesting(false)` and every non-control part of the row does the
    same. Without that the surface would swallow menu-bar clicks either side of the
    notch for ten minutes at a time. `AppState.ambientRowTakesClicks` is the one
    predicate the window guard and the view's own `allowsHitTesting` both read.
  - **⚠️ The trailing cluster needs its own wing allowance**
    (`NotchSurfaceLayout.stateChipAllowance`). The wings are symmetric and were
    sized to a bare label; the chipped form spends its wing on the chip's padding,
    the gap to the orb, and an agent's stop button between them, so sized to the
    label alone the orb was pushed against the band's rounded corner and drew
    clipped.
  - **⚠️ `extraWing` must never read `ambientSlot`.** `ambientSlot` carries
    `rowLabelMaxWidth`, which is computed from the wing, which is what `extraWing`
    feeds — closing that loop is a stack overflow at layout time, not a compile
    error. It cost a SIGSEGV in the headless renderer on the first frame that had
    both an ambient item and a state word. `ownStateLabel` exists to break it, and
    `stateLabel` is `widerLabel(ownStateLabel, ambientLabel)` because the symmetric
    wings have to fit whichever side is longer.
  - Renders: `pill-11-now-meeting`, `pill-11b-now-overdue`,
    `pill-11c-now-while-dictating`, `pill-11d-now-while-agent-works`.

  **The band's state is one named value, and it lights the surface.** `NotchActivity` (`UI/NotchActivity.swift`) names what the band is doing — `preparing · listening · transcribing · polishing · polished · delivered · failed · idle` — and `DictationPillContent` resolves it **once** per frame (`NotchActivity.resolve(from:)`, `@MainActor` since `AppState` is) and hands it to both `DictationStatusView` and `NotchGlow`. Before this, the orb figure, the state word, and the trailing bloom each re-derived the state from `AppState` separately and could disagree. `resolve`'s branch order mirrors `DictationStatusView`'s rendering order exactly (download → failure → polished beat → live work → delivered) — **keep them in sync**, or the band lights one colour while rendering another. It's gated on `bandIsBanner`, not `bandIsDictation`: a failure and a model download are dictation states that deserve their light even though neither carries a transcript.
  - **`NotchGlow` (`UI/NotchGlow.swift`) owns all of the band's light** — one source (an elliptical bloom anchored to the state's own lit element: trailing for the orb states, centre for the delivered checkmark and the failure line) plus a bottom-lip wash masked to fade before it reaches the words. Applied inside the `NotchShape` clip in `DictationPillContent`, over the fill and under the content; outside the clip it spills past the band's rounded corners. **Hues follow §1 and are load-bearing:** `listening` is the only ember state (your voice, live); every machine state is signal; failure is danger. **Strengths are deliberately low (0.10–0.22)** — a saturated hue at low alpha over pure black reads as dirt, not light, and ember in particular turns brown; the first attempt lit four places at 0.30 and looked like a smudged band. **Nothing pulses**: the record dot's 2.6s breathe is the system's only perpetual motion and a breathing notch would spend that meaning twice. Banners get `.idle` (matte) on purpose — they carry their own colour and meaning.
  - `NotchTranscriptRow` no longer draws its own ember bloom; it takes a `tint` for the `icon` path so the polished beat's sparkles glyph matches the signal glow instead of claiming the rewrite was yours.

  **Line breaking is ours, not `Text`'s** (`NotchTranscriptModel` + `NotchTextMetrics`) — used by the one band that still carries text, the **polished beat**, which is a **rolling three-line window**: each word fades in on its own, lines fill top-to-bottom, and once the third is full the block slides upward with older text clipped behind a soft top fade. A single wrapped `Text` can't grow the band per line, slide old lines out, or animate words in individually, so the words are wrapped explicitly: `NotchTextMetrics` measures advance widths with an `NSFont` matched to `Typography.notchBody` (memoised in an `NSCache`) and `NotchTranscriptModel.wrap` greedily breaks them into `[Range<Int>]` lines — pure, and covered by `NotchTranscriptModelTests`. **The model is resolved once, by `DictationPillContent`,** and handed down to the row, so the band's thickness and the row's line count come from the same wrap (no measurement round-trip, no one-frame lag). It's resolved at the *transcript* width unconditionally — that looks circular since the width depends on whether there's a transcript, but an empty model always means a narrower surface, so the wide width is correct whenever the answer is used. **`NotchTranscriptRow.textWidth` must charge `gutter` three times** (both `HStack` gaps around the `Spacer` plus its `minLength`) — budget it once and a line is measured to fit a column it never gets, so `Text` truncates a whole word mid-line ("transc…"). That was invisible while the bar was wide enough that nothing wrapped.

  **The surface width is a three-step ladder** (`NotchSurfaceWidth`): `.glyph` (`glyphSideExtension`, a badge hugging the notch — the delivered checkmark), `.banner` (`sideExtension`, what every hint is written against), `.wide` (`wideSideExtension`, the dictation bar — the state at one end, the orb at the other). `wideSideExtension` is **sized to the longest fixed state word** ("Dictating (hands-free)", ~139pt) plus `NotchTranscriptRow.horizontalPadding`, not to a sentence of transcript: since the band no longer streams the live words, anything wider is just empty black either side of the camera housing. The `pill-0b-listening-hands-free` snapshot is the regression check on that — if it truncates or runs under the housing, the wing is too short. It is a **floor, not the width**: the assistant's captions name a user-chosen connector, so `surfaceWidth(for:_:stateLabel:)` grows the wing to fit them (see the assistant bullet below); pass no label when you want the base width, as `panelSize`'s caller and the transcript wrap both do. Dictation takes `.wide` from the **first** beat, because the state line is what the bar carries; only the delivered checkmark stays a badge (`DictationPillContent.isDeliveredBadge`, which mirrors `DictationStatusView`'s branch order). **The approval card is the one banner that also takes `.wide`** — it carries three buttons beside its two lines, and at banner width the words and the answers were competing for the same ~200pt. `.wide` is the one surface long enough to run off a small display, so it's the only one clamped to `NotchGeometry.screenWidth` (minus `wideScreenInset` each side; `screenWidth` is 0 = unclamped for the headless `.none` geometry). `panelSize` is always sized for `.wide` and `DictationPillContent` frames the black surface to the per-state width, centered on the notch, so the panel never clips; width *and* thickness changes are animated, so the notch reads as opening up and deepening a row at a time rather than jumping. The orb's figure tracks the work: `.listening` (audio-reactive wave) while recording, `.working` (orbits) while preparing/finalizing, `.thinking` (the `solving` scramble) while the optional qwen polish runs (`AppState.isPolishing`, flipped inside `DictationViewModel.llmRefined`). When a polish lands and actually reached the user, `polishedText`/`polishedAt` hold the rewritten line in the notch for a beat (`polishedBeatDuration`) so the substitution isn't invisible — set from the call sites that know delivery succeeded, never from `llmRefined` itself (a refine that failed to apply in place must not be claimed). `NotchUndeliveredBanner` carries the transcript (`AppState.undeliveredText`) plus a **Copy** button, so a dictation with nowhere to paste is recoverable without trusting the clipboard; a late polish swaps that text (and the clipboard, only if our own string is still on it) for the better wording. It's the third interactive banner, so it's in both `DictationPillContent.allowsHitTesting` and the `setInteractive` call in the AppDelegate refresh loop. `NotchDueReminderBanner` is the fourth (a checkbox that ticks both ways; tapping the text → Notes & Reminders): a reminder the user scheduled announces itself **here** rather than in Notification Centre, so it sits near the top of the ladder — above every passive hint, below only the approval card, the "when?" quick-prompt and the undelivered hint. Its window is paused rather than run down while it's suppressed (`AppState.canShowDueReminderBanner`); see **Scheduled reminders** in the root `CLAUDE.md`.
- **While the assistant works, the bar names the connector it's waiting on.** The chord suppresses the paste, so between letting go and the answer landing the band is the only thing saying the words went anywhere — and it said "Working on it" for the whole run, up to `AgentLoop.budget` (30s) across as many as `maxIterations` tool calls, which can't distinguish a calendar read from a Slack post. `AgentLoop.onStep` reports each call *before* it runs (reporting on completion would name a connector it has already finished waiting on), `DictationViewModel` writes it to `AppState.agentActivity` — the loop reports, the view model writes, so the one-writer rule survives the callback — and `NotchActivity.label` prefers `AgentActivity.caption` over the generic line. It only ever replaces the assistant's own caption; a step left set can't re-caption an ordinary dictation. `.thinking` is reported **once**, not before every generation: after a call returns the model is reasoning about *that* connector's result, so holding its caption is calmer than flipping back to the generic line between every step. **The tool's own name is never shown** — a notch reading `list_calendar_events` is the same leak the approval card's raw arguments were, so an unrecognised tool falls back to "Working on it" (`AgentActivityTests`).
  - **This is why the bar's width now follows its own state word.** `wideSideExtension` was a constant sized to the longest fixed caption; these carry a **user-chosen connector name**, and in the row form the leading label lives entirely in the wing — so a caption longer than the wing doesn't truncate, it runs into the region the camera housing physically covers and its tail is simply not on screen. `NotchSurfaceLayout.wideWing(forStateLabel:)` measures the caption and grows the wing, capped at `maxStateLabelWing`; **`panelSize` is computed from that cap, not from the base wing**, because the panel is sized once at window creation and a band wider than its panel is clipped by its own window. The cap exists because the panel takes clicks while a banner is up, so a bar growing toward screen-wide would swallow menu-bar clicks. Past the cap, `rowLabelMaxWidth` truncates the label with an ellipsis at the wing's edge rather than letting it slide under the housing. `pill-7b`…`pill-7e` are the renders; `NotchSurfaceLayoutTests` pins the arithmetic.
- **Assistant preferences live on Settings; only the grant list stays with the connections.** `AssistantSettingsView` (the "Let it use your connectors" switch + the whole Spoken-answers stack) sits in `GeneralSettingsView` right after Smart cleanup, which is the same on-device model those answers run on. It was at the bottom of the Connectors page — two ordinary preferences on a page whose job is managing accounts, and a Settings page missing the place people look for them. What stayed is `ConnectorPermissionsSection`: a grant binds to `(tool, instance, target)`, so the list is meaningless away from the accounts it names, and it hides itself entirely when there are no grants. **Scheduled automations are gone** — the section, the store, the scheduler, and the second "also read scheduled answers" switch that existed only because an automation could talk unprompted.
- **The approval card is the only band with a caller suspended behind it** (`NotchApprovalBanner`), and everything unusual about it follows from that. It's first in `DictationPillContent.isExpanded` — it used to be absent, and was on screen only because `AppState.isPolishing` happened to be holding the band open for the agent loop around it, so a card raised outside that window would have left a write waiting on a question nobody was shown until it timed out and denied itself. It passes `textGivesWayToTrailing: true` to `NotchBannerRow`, because its payload is dictated and therefore unbounded: with the row's default intrinsic width, a long message body ran off both edges of the band and pushed **Once / Always / No** out past the clip, where they were invisible and unclickable. And its words come from `ApprovalCopy`, not from the arguments: the card used to render the router's own stamps (`end: …+05:30 · start: …+05:30 · title: Work · when: tomorrow at nine` under "Add an event to Personal on Personal"), which is one time in three notations under a name said twice. `ApprovalCopy` **presents** the payload rather than selecting from it — anything it doesn't recognise is still shown raw, so a new argument on an existing tool degrades to the old form instead of disappearing from a consent card. `pill-9-approval-calendar` / `pill-9b-approval-long-message` are the regression checks; the copy itself is pure and clock-injected (`ApprovalCopyTests`).
- `Theme.swift` is the **"Organic"** design system: a paper-cream ground washed with soft accent blooms (`WarmBackground`), frosted-glass surfaces (`.glassCard` / `.glassTile` / `.glassPanel`; the legacy `.card()` is an alias so old call-sites inherit glass), a toasted-orange accent + sage secondary with full tonal ramps (`Theme.Accent` / `Theme.Sage` / `Theme.Neutral`), a `Space` scale, rounder radii, warm `Shadow` tokens, semantic status colors, a separate always-ink `Theme.Notch` sub-palette for the bezel bands (the app itself is **light-only** — no dark mode, no appearance preference), `Motion` springs (`respecting(reduceMotion:)`), and `Typography` — **Caprasimo** (display/headings, `Typography.heading`) over **Figtree** (body/UI, `Typography.sans`), both bundled under `Resources/Fonts/` and registered at launch by `BrandFont.registerAll()` (falls back to system faces if missing); every token is `relativeTo:` a text style for Dynamic Type. Shared primitives live in `Settings/SettingsComponents.swift` (`SettingsCard`, `StatTile`, `Chip`, `IconButton`, accessible `ThemeToggle`). `DesignTokens.swift` holds `BrandAsset`/`BrandLogo`. New UI should use these tokens/primitives rather than ad-hoc literals.
- **`UI/Components/` is the enforceable component layer** — the spec lives in `docs/07-design-system.md` (§8a is normative for buttons), the tokens in `Theme.swift`, and the components here. A view reaching for a raw hex or a bare `.buttonStyle(.plain)` with hand-drawn chrome is visibly off-system.
  - **`ButtonStyles.swift` — the button ladder.** Six rungs plus an icon button, in descending emphasis, each with its intent in a doc comment: `.primaryButton()` (**one per view**) · `.secondaryButton()` · `.outlinedButton()` · `.ghostButton()` · `.textButton()` (no background in any state) · `.destructiveButton()` · `.iconButton(size:tone:tooltip:)`. All share one body (`LadderButtonBody`) that owns the pill geometry, the 2px hover lift on the house curve, `Theme.StateLayer` hover/press strengths, the MD3 disabled treatment, Reduce Motion, and the pointer cursor — so add a rung to `ButtonRung`, don't write another `ButtonStyle`. Primary carries press feedback on the ember ramp (`Ember.bright` → `Ember.deep`) rather than a state layer, because a tint over ember muddies the hue.
  - **`.onDarkSurface()`** marks a subtree that is always ink — the notch and onboarding bands, which sit on the physical bezel. It flips the whole ladder onto `Theme.Notch` tokens. Apply it **once at the band's root** (`NotchOnboardingView` does); without it the app's light tokens draw near-black ink on black. **The app itself is light-only** (no dark mode, no appearance preference — `NSApp.appearance` is pinned to `.aqua` in `applicationDidFinishLaunching`), so this flag and `Theme.Notch` are the *only* always-ink path; the three notch panels also pin `.darkAqua` on their own windows. Don't reintroduce a mode-aware token helper — every `Theme` token is a plain constant. The dictation pill's own inline chrome and the notch banners' pills deliberately stay hand-tuned — they're measured against `NotchGeometry` and a capsule swap changes the band's metrics.
  - **`CursorBridges.swift` — `.pointerCursor()` / `.iBeamCursor()` / `.nativeTooltip(_:)`.** AppKit cursor rects and `NSView.toolTip`, because `NSCursor.push/pop` inside `.onHover` leaks a pushed cursor when the exit callback is coalesced away, and SwiftUI's `.help()` installs a competing tracking area that leaves hover highlights stuck. `.pointerCursor()` reads `\.isEnabled` itself, so it must sit **inside** any `.disabled(…)` (environment only flows down) and at the **end** of the visual chain (the cursor rect covers the frame *at the point it's applied* — put it before `.padding` and only the bare label gets the hand). A tooltip is not an accessibility label; set both.
  - **⚠️ All three bridges are omitted under `\.isSnapshot`.** `ImageRenderer` cannot draw `NSViewRepresentable` and substitutes a red "unsupported" placeholder — since these overlays cover their whole frame, leaving them in blanks out every button in every snapshot PNG. Any new `NSViewRepresentable` overlay needs the same gate (`AppKitOverlay` already applies it).
