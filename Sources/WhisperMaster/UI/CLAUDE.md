# UI (`Sources/WhisperMaster/UI/`)

Design-system and notch-surface rules for this directory. The component spec is
`docs/07-design-system.md` (§8a normative for buttons); tokens live in `Theme.swift`.

### UI

- **Quick actions on hover** (`UI/QuickActions/`) — resting the pointer on the notch opens a band holding what's due and the notes you touched last, plus the three things you'd otherwise open the window for. Four pieces: `NotchHoverGesture` (pure dwell/grace recogniser, unit-tested — the notch is crossed constantly on the way to the menu bar, so it opens only after the pointer *rests* for `openDwell` and closes only after `closeGrace` off it, which is what covers the gap a hand crosses on the way down into the band), `NotchQuickActionsModel` (what the columns hold + `canOpen`), `NotchQuickActionsView`, `NotchQuickActionsWindow`. Two rules make it safe to put a panel on the notch: **it is only on screen while open** — nothing of ours sits over the menu bar otherwise, so a click up there still reaches the menu bar exactly as before — and **hover is polled, not tracked** (`NSEvent.mouseLocation` on a 0.06 s tick against `NotchQuickActionsLayout.hoverZone`; with no panel on screen there is no view to receive `mouseEntered`, and a tracking area on a panel that resizes under the cursor doesn't reliably re-fire).
  - **The target is a block, not a strip, and the tick is part of the feel.** The zone is the notch body plus `hoverSideExtension` wings, running from the top of the screen down through the menu bar and `approachDepth` past it. Both halves of that were the "it only answers at the notch's bottom edge" bug: a 10pt wing meant aiming at the camera housing itself, and a zone only as deep as the notch missed a pointer that decelerated to rest a few points *under* the menu bar, which is where a hand travelling up naturally stops. It stops short of the menu bar's own items either side — reaching for the clock must not open it — and `NotchQuickActionsZoneTests` pins both edges of that trade. The poll interval is pure latency on top of the dwell (the pointer arrives *between* samples), so it's small: comparing a point against two rects costs nothing, and at 0.15 s a third of a second of slop read as the notch ignoring you. While the panel is open, `isPointerEngaged` unions the band (plus `exitMargin`) with the notch zone, so the whole travel from notch to band keeps it up and the grace is a safety net rather than the thing holding it open. It yields the notch to the dictation surface via `AppState.notchIsOccupied` (generous on purpose — when in doubt dictation wins) and to onboarding via `suspend()`/`resume()` from the AppDelegate. **It composes nothing itself**: a non-activating panel on the bezel has no key window, so "New note"/"New reminder" set `AppState.requestedNotesComposer` and the real editor opens in Settings. Depth is content-driven (`thickness(rows:)`) so an empty account gets a strip, not a slab. Its one inline mutation — the reminder checkbox — **ticks both ways**: a row ticked during a glance stays in place, struck through, until the panel closes, so a mis-click is undoable on the bezel (`NotchQuickActionsModel.ticked` holds the pre-tick snapshots; see **The notch checkboxes** in the root `CLAUDE.md` for why an id isn't enough). Hard off-switch: `AppState.quickActionsEnabled` (**on by default**).
- **Onboarding happens in the notch** (`UI/Onboarding/`), not in a window — first-run setup is presented on exactly the surface dictation will live on, so the user learns where to look while they're granting. `NotchOnboardingWindow` is a borderless `.nonactivatingPanel` anchored top-center (same level / collection behavior / pinned-dark appearance as `DictationPillWindow`, but it **takes clicks** and is sized exactly to its one fixed surface via `NotchOnboardingLayout`); it hosts `NotchOnboardingView`, a molded `NotchShape` band that drops out of the notch holding one ask at a time: step dots, the orb, one headline, one sub-line, one button (plus gear → Settings and × → dismiss). Four beats (`NotchOnboardingStep`): **microphone** → **accessibility** → **open at login** → **ready** (the shortcut + the hand-off). The login-item beat is here rather than only in Settings because a shortcut that does nothing after a reboot reads as the app being broken; it treats `.requiresApproval` as its own ask (see **Open at login** in the root `CLAUDE.md`) and never counts it as done. `NotchOnboardingModel` (`@MainActor @Observable`, owned by the window) holds the flow: it polls the two grants (neither posts a change notification — 0.75 s tick + `didBecomeActive`, same as the old wizard), auto-advances a beat after each ask is satisfied, and runs the mic check.
  - **The orb is the flow's only moving part — there is no waveform.** It **thinks** (`solving`) while an ask is outstanding, **works** (`orbits`) while a system prompt is up, and **listens** (`wave`, driven by the real mic level) once it can hear. That's what makes the microphone beat proof instead of a claim: it won't auto-advance on the grant alone, it waits until it has actually heard a voice (`heardVoice`), with a "Continue" ghost for anyone who'd rather not speak. `OrbView` gained a `preset` knob for this — the notch band uses the inline-scale `small` tuning, the onboarding hero uses `large`.
  - The mic check owns **its own `MicrophoneCaptureService`** (each one owns an `AVAudioEngine`, so it must not share the view model's) and runs **only** during the microphone beat, which is the one place the copy says we're listening; `close()`/`teardown()` releases it. `AppDelegate.showOnboarding` **hides the dictation pill** while the band is up (both anchor to the notch, and the launch-time engine download would otherwise draw its progress band straight through this one) and `dismissOnboarding()` gives it back.
  - Persistence is unchanged: `OnboardingStep`/`OnboardingProgress` still record "this account has onboarded" (per-Clerk-user, with the legacy-id migration), so `NotchOnboardingStep` is presentation only. Voice engine is **not** part of the flow — `prepareDefaultEngineOnLaunch()` runs post-auth in parallel and only downloads when `TranscriberEngine.isInstalled` is false; the last beat just tells the truth about its progress instead of claiming "all set".
- `SettingsView` — the **main window** (`SettingsView.swift`). A floating frosted-glass panel (`.glassPanel`) over `WarmBackground`, with a slim sidebar (brand lockup, four nav pills, a push-to-talk mic card, the account row) beside a scrolling content pane. The sidebar is **four primary sections** — Today / Notes & Reminders / Connectors / Settings (`SettingsSection.primary`); the six former sections (Insights, Voice engine, History, Permissions, Nearby Macs, About = `SettingsSection.secondary`) are folded into a "More" list on the Settings screen and open as sub-pages with a back affordance (a secondary page highlights `settings` in the sidebar via `sidebarParent`). **There is no Account page** — the sidebar's bottom account row carries a gear button to the right of the avatar/name that opens `AccountPopover` (`Settings/AccountPopover.swift`: email, account id in small mono under it, joined date, sign out with an inline confirm — a `confirmationDialog` would dismiss the popover). Clerk state is read the same way as `AuthGateView` (`@Environment(Clerk.self)` on a live-only subview, snapshot path uses a static stand-in; `AccountIdentity` derives name/email/avatar for both the row and the popup). `TodayView` is the greeting + **real-data-only** cards (live EventKit agenda via `CalendarConnector`/`DaySummaryService`, due reminders from `NotesStore`); no weather/mail/AI-briefing (no backend). When the model isn't installed at launch, `autoFocusSetupIfNeeded` jumps to the Engine sub-page; `SetupBanner` renders atop panels while prep is in flight.
- `DictationPillWindow` / `DictationPillContent` — floating pill showing audio level + transcription state on a pitch-black notch band. `state.hidePillWhenIdle` controls visibility between recordings. **The band does NOT show the live transcript.** It reports the *state* of a dictation, never the words as they stream: reading your own speech back off the bezel pulls your eyes away from whatever you're dictating into, and the words are already landing there. So `DictationPillContent.transcriptModel` is empty for the whole recording → finalizing → polishing stretch — which is what keeps the surface in its slim menu-bar row (`isNotchRow`) for an entire dictation — and non-empty **only** for the finished polished beat. Don't re-wire `state.transcript` into it "so you can see what it heard"; the history list and the target app are where the words live. **The bar's two ends are fixed and never swap:** the state in words sits at the **leading** edge — "Dictating", or **"Dictating (hands-free)"** in toggle mode, where the band staying open isn't explained by a held key (`DictationStatusView.liveStateWord`, also the VoiceOver label) — and the `OrbView` at the **trailing** edge (40pt, `NotchTranscriptRow.orbDiameter`, which `bottomThickness` tracks so a lone orb is never clipped). The light behind the orb is `NotchGlow`'s, not the row's (below).

  **The band's state is one named value, and it lights the surface.** `NotchActivity` (`UI/NotchActivity.swift`) names what the band is doing — `preparing · listening · transcribing · polishing · polished · delivered · failed · idle` — and `DictationPillContent` resolves it **once** per frame (`NotchActivity.resolve(from:)`, `@MainActor` since `AppState` is) and hands it to both `DictationStatusView` and `NotchGlow`. Before this, the orb figure, the state word, and the trailing bloom each re-derived the state from `AppState` separately and could disagree. `resolve`'s branch order mirrors `DictationStatusView`'s rendering order exactly (download → failure → polished beat → live work → delivered) — **keep them in sync**, or the band lights one colour while rendering another. It's gated on `bandIsBanner`, not `bandIsDictation`: a failure and a model download are dictation states that deserve their light even though neither carries a transcript.
  - **`NotchGlow` (`UI/NotchGlow.swift`) owns all of the band's light** — one source (an elliptical bloom anchored to the state's own lit element: trailing for the orb states, centre for the delivered checkmark and the failure line) plus a bottom-lip wash masked to fade before it reaches the words. Applied inside the `NotchShape` clip in `DictationPillContent`, over the fill and under the content; outside the clip it spills past the band's rounded corners. **Hues follow §1 and are load-bearing:** `listening` is the only ember state (your voice, live); every machine state is signal; failure is danger. **Strengths are deliberately low (0.10–0.22)** — a saturated hue at low alpha over pure black reads as dirt, not light, and ember in particular turns brown; the first attempt lit four places at 0.30 and looked like a smudged band. **Nothing pulses**: the record dot's 2.6s breathe is the system's only perpetual motion and a breathing notch would spend that meaning twice. Banners get `.idle` (matte) on purpose — they carry their own colour and meaning.
  - `NotchTranscriptRow` no longer draws its own ember bloom; it takes a `tint` for the `icon` path so the polished beat's sparkles glyph matches the signal glow instead of claiming the rewrite was yours.

  **Line breaking is ours, not `Text`'s** (`NotchTranscriptModel` + `NotchTextMetrics`) — used by the one band that still carries text, the **polished beat**, which is a **rolling three-line window**: each word fades in on its own, lines fill top-to-bottom, and once the third is full the block slides upward with older text clipped behind a soft top fade. A single wrapped `Text` can't grow the band per line, slide old lines out, or animate words in individually, so the words are wrapped explicitly: `NotchTextMetrics` measures advance widths with an `NSFont` matched to `Typography.notchBody` (memoised in an `NSCache`) and `NotchTranscriptModel.wrap` greedily breaks them into `[Range<Int>]` lines — pure, and covered by `NotchTranscriptModelTests`. **The model is resolved once, by `DictationPillContent`,** and handed down to the row, so the band's thickness and the row's line count come from the same wrap (no measurement round-trip, no one-frame lag). It's resolved at the *transcript* width unconditionally — that looks circular since the width depends on whether there's a transcript, but an empty model always means a narrower surface, so the wide width is correct whenever the answer is used. **`NotchTranscriptRow.textWidth` must charge `gutter` three times** (both `HStack` gaps around the `Spacer` plus its `minLength`) — budget it once and a line is measured to fit a column it never gets, so `Text` truncates a whole word mid-line ("transc…"). That was invisible while the bar was wide enough that nothing wrapped.

  **The surface width is a three-step ladder** (`NotchSurfaceWidth`): `.glyph` (`glyphSideExtension`, a badge hugging the notch — the delivered checkmark), `.banner` (`sideExtension`, what every hint is written against), `.wide` (`wideSideExtension`, the dictation bar — the state at one end, the orb at the other). `wideSideExtension` is **sized to the longest state word** ("Dictating (hands-free)", ~145pt) plus `NotchTranscriptRow.horizontalPadding`, not to a sentence of transcript: since the band no longer streams the live words, anything wider is just empty black either side of the camera housing. The `pill-0b-listening-hands-free` snapshot is the regression check on that — if it truncates or runs under the housing, the wing is too short. Dictation takes `.wide` from the **first** beat, because the state line is what the bar carries; only the delivered checkmark stays a badge (`DictationPillContent.isDeliveredBadge`, which mirrors `DictationStatusView`'s branch order). `.wide` is the one surface long enough to run off a small display, so it's the only one clamped to `NotchGeometry.screenWidth` (minus `wideScreenInset` each side; `screenWidth` is 0 = unclamped for the headless `.none` geometry). `panelSize` is always sized for `.wide` and `DictationPillContent` frames the black surface to the per-state width, centered on the notch, so the panel never clips; width *and* thickness changes are animated, so the notch reads as opening up and deepening a row at a time rather than jumping. The orb's figure tracks the work: `.listening` (audio-reactive wave) while recording, `.working` (orbits) while preparing/finalizing, `.thinking` (the `solving` scramble) while the optional qwen polish runs (`AppState.isPolishing`, flipped inside `DictationViewModel.llmRefined`). When a polish lands and actually reached the user, `polishedText`/`polishedAt` hold the rewritten line in the notch for a beat (`polishedBeatDuration`) so the substitution isn't invisible — set from the call sites that know delivery succeeded, never from `llmRefined` itself (a refine that failed to apply in place must not be claimed). `NotchUndeliveredBanner` carries the transcript (`AppState.undeliveredText`) plus a **Copy** button, so a dictation with nowhere to paste is recoverable without trusting the clipboard; a late polish swaps that text (and the clipboard, only if our own string is still on it) for the better wording. It's the third interactive banner, so it's in both `DictationPillContent.allowsHitTesting` and the `setInteractive` call in the AppDelegate refresh loop. `NotchDueReminderBanner` is the fourth (a checkbox that ticks both ways; tapping the text → Notes & Reminders): a reminder the user scheduled announces itself **here** rather than in Notification Centre, so it sits near the top of the ladder — above every passive hint, below only the approval card, the "when?" quick-prompt and the undelivered hint. Its window is paused rather than run down while it's suppressed (`AppState.canShowDueReminderBanner`); see **Scheduled reminders** in the root `CLAUDE.md`.
- `Theme.swift` is the **"Organic"** design system: a paper-cream ground washed with soft accent blooms (`WarmBackground`), frosted-glass surfaces (`.glassCard` / `.glassTile` / `.glassPanel`; the legacy `.card()` is an alias so old call-sites inherit glass), a toasted-orange accent + sage secondary with full tonal ramps (`Theme.Accent` / `Theme.Sage` / `Theme.Neutral`), a `Space` scale, rounder radii, warm `Shadow` tokens, semantic status colors, a separate on-dark `Theme.Notch` sub-palette, `Motion` springs (`respecting(reduceMotion:)`), and `Typography` — **Caprasimo** (display/headings, `Typography.heading`) over **Figtree** (body/UI, `Typography.sans`), both bundled under `Resources/Fonts/` and registered at launch by `BrandFont.registerAll()` (falls back to system faces if missing); every token is `relativeTo:` a text style for Dynamic Type. Shared primitives live in `Settings/SettingsComponents.swift` (`SettingsCard`, `StatTile`, `Chip`, `IconButton`, accessible `ThemeToggle`). `DesignTokens.swift` holds `BrandAsset`/`BrandLogo`. New UI should use these tokens/primitives rather than ad-hoc literals.
- **`UI/Components/` is the enforceable component layer** — the spec lives in `docs/07-design-system.md` (§8a is normative for buttons), the tokens in `Theme.swift`, and the components here. A view reaching for a raw hex or a bare `.buttonStyle(.plain)` with hand-drawn chrome is visibly off-system.
  - **`ButtonStyles.swift` — the button ladder.** Six rungs plus an icon button, in descending emphasis, each with its intent in a doc comment: `.primaryButton()` (**one per view**) · `.secondaryButton()` · `.outlinedButton()` · `.ghostButton()` · `.textButton()` (no background in any state) · `.destructiveButton()` · `.iconButton(size:tone:tooltip:)`. All share one body (`LadderButtonBody`) that owns the pill geometry, the 2px hover lift on the house curve, `Theme.StateLayer` hover/press strengths, the MD3 disabled treatment, Reduce Motion, and the pointer cursor — so add a rung to `ButtonRung`, don't write another `ButtonStyle`. Primary carries press feedback on the ember ramp (`Ember.bright` → `Ember.deep`) rather than a state layer, because a tint over ember muddies the hue.
  - **`.onDarkSurface()`** marks a subtree that is always ink whatever the appearance setting is — the notch and onboarding bands, which sit on the physical bezel. It flips the whole ladder onto `Theme.Notch` tokens. Apply it **once at the band's root** (`NotchOnboardingView` does); without it a light-mode window draws near-black ink on black. The dictation pill's own inline chrome and the notch banners' pills deliberately stay hand-tuned — they're measured against `NotchGeometry` and a capsule swap changes the band's metrics.
  - **`CursorBridges.swift` — `.pointerCursor()` / `.iBeamCursor()` / `.nativeTooltip(_:)`.** AppKit cursor rects and `NSView.toolTip`, because `NSCursor.push/pop` inside `.onHover` leaks a pushed cursor when the exit callback is coalesced away, and SwiftUI's `.help()` installs a competing tracking area that leaves hover highlights stuck. `.pointerCursor()` reads `\.isEnabled` itself, so it must sit **inside** any `.disabled(…)` (environment only flows down) and at the **end** of the visual chain (the cursor rect covers the frame *at the point it's applied* — put it before `.padding` and only the bare label gets the hand). A tooltip is not an accessibility label; set both.
  - **⚠️ All three bridges are omitted under `\.isSnapshot`.** `ImageRenderer` cannot draw `NSViewRepresentable` and substitutes a red "unsupported" placeholder — since these overlays cover their whole frame, leaving them in blanks out every button in every snapshot PNG. Any new `NSViewRepresentable` overlay needs the same gate (`AppKitOverlay` already applies it).
